# The compliance calendar

Scope: the generated compliance calendar (T3.2: `delivery.obligation_template` / `_exception` / `obligation_state` 0052, `obligation_instance` 0053), closing a filing (0054), missed deadlines and resolution (T3.3, 0055/0056), editing rules (T6.4), effective-dated supersession (0057, 0303), rule packs, service-linked rules (T6.16), filing zones, and international filings (S3). Escalation of missed work is `escalation-chaser-nudge.md`; the filing's task is `work-items-and-review.md`; day arithmetic is `dates-and-timezones.md`.

## Generated, never typed (T3.2)

- **AN OBLIGATION IS NOT A TASK.** The obligation is what the LAW requires of the ENTITY (Kaveri Foods must file GSTR-3B for August by 20 September); the task (`delivery.work_item`) is what somebody at the firm must DO. Merged, the calendar could only be typed (the Excel this product replaces). Nobody adds a row: it falls out of which entities exist, what each bought and what the law says. **There is no create endpoint for an instance and there must not be one.**
- **Own tables, NOT a config-registry kind** (the deviation CLAUDE.md requires to be written down). Weighed and set aside: the generator reads EVERY rule in one set-based pass (registry answers one key at a time); an exception needs a real FK to its rule; the shape ran in the approved demo. **Cost paid by hand:** `rule_pack` is part of the rule's IDENTITY (unique key `(tenant, jurisdiction, name, rule_pack)`), so a revision is a NEW ROW; `supersedes_id` is the chain; retiring is `is_active` (old row readable forever); an instance PINS its template. Without all four, "why was this due then?" is unanswerable (same deviation as `staff_grade`).
- **`due_rule` IS NULLABLE: the most consequential decision here.** The firm's applicability sheet has 55 obligations; ten are formulas. The rest depend on an AGM date, a state, or a holiday calendar nothing here holds. The demo handed them back in a log; **a log is not an answer**, so all are ROWS: computable ones carry a due rule and generate, the rest carry a mandatory `unresolved_reason` in the firm's own terms. `domain/obligation/rulepack-in.ts`: **a due date is encoded ONLY where the firm's own sheet states one** (MSME-1 is genuinely due 31 Oct and 30 Apr and the pack does not say so). `tests/obligation/rulepack.test.ts` PINS the generating count at ten and names all ten. A fake VALUE is survivable; a fake RULE is not.
- **Date arithmetic is pure, no `Date`** (`domain/obligation/model.ts`, `YYYY-MM-DD`). The fiscal year is on the RULE (4 for India); quarters on the calendar is the mistake that looks right three times out of four.
- **The exception is a ROW against the rule, keyed on the month the PERIOD ends in**, not the month due (the due month is what the exception changes). March's TDS is due 30 April, the other eleven on the 7th; reading only the normal half is right eleven months and quietly wrong every April. Withdrawing = `is_active = false`; `bib_app` has no DELETE on any of the four tables.
- **Two dates:** `statutory_due_on` is the law's (missing it is the client's money); `internal_target_on` is T-X, the firm's own, the actionable one; every screen orders by it.
- **ANY DATE-CHANGING EDIT RE-DATES WHAT IS ALREADY GENERATED** (`listUnstarted` + `restate`, T6.4): the sweep materialises a year ahead and generation is idempotent on `(tenant, lineage, entity, period_start)` (0303). It moves only rows nobody has started (a task on a desk keeps its date) and the caller REPORTS how many it could not touch. It re-derives with **`scheduleFor`, the generator's own function**, never arithmetic in an UPDATE (a second implementation parts company the moment an exception is involved). **It restates the STATUTORY date** (reverse of the lead-time-only version): when the FORMULA changes, the old statutory date was this system's guess from a vendor's document and the new one is the firm's answer.
- **States are ROWS and NOTHING compares a `code`** (0050's argument; the demo's sweep looked up `'pending'`/`'in_progress'` literally). Meaning lives in `is_initial` / `is_terminal` / `needs_decision`; "can this still be missed" is derived from the last two.
- **Generation scope is DECIDED: only where an engagement exists.** `engagement_id` stays nullable so the compliance-engine reading (generate for every entity; every uncovered obligation is a sales lead) is a config change, not a migration; the firm has not been asked. **The floor matters as much as the scope:** generation reaches 90 days back, and `CalendarSubject.effectiveFrom` stops that reach-back inventing missed deadlines (onboard 400 clients without it and a partner's queue fills with thousands nobody had).
- **Thresholds are still NOT built, now a decision:** the commonest applicability rule is a threshold, `StepOperator` has no `gte`/`lte` for the same reason (no stored turnover / employee count / paid-up capital). Under the decided scope "does Tax Audit apply?" is answered by the client having BOUGHT it. The threshold layer belongs with the compliance-engine reading; every seeded rule carries the sheet's threshold text in `applicabilityNote`.

### Closing a filing: "terminal" is the wrong rule on BOTH sides (0054)

T3.2 READ `filed_on` in four places and WROTE it in none (`obligation.filed` had no writer). "The work item reached a terminal state, so it is filed" is ambiguous both ways:

- 0050 seeds TWO terminal work-item states (`completed` AND `cancelled`); a cancelled task (client left, entity deregistered, raised twice) must not record that a statutory return was FILED (a false statement about a filing). 0052 seeds TWO terminal obligation states (`filed` AND `resolved`; `resolved` is T3.3's missed-deadline end).
- So each side carries ONE flag: **`work_item_state.is_success`** and **`obligation_state.is_filed`**. Flags not codes (a firm renaming Filed to "Submitted"); `filedState()` mirrors `initialState()`; a partial unique index caps `is_filed` at one per firm; a CHECK refuses either on a non-terminal state. **Both default FALSE**: a firm's new terminal state leaves an obligation OPEN rather than recording a filing that never happened.
- **`ObligationFiling.reconcileFiling` is the only writer and it RECONCILES**: it takes "is this discharged" (terminal AND successful) and makes the obligation agree; `moveToState` calls it on every move. Idempotent (the guard is `WHERE filed_on IS NULL`). **A reopened or cancelled task UN-FILES** (filed is terminal, so `canStillBeMissed` would be false forever and T3.3 could never flag it); its own verb `obligation.filing_reversed`.
- **The cycle is broken in `setup/`:** the calendar raises a task (`WorkItemRaiser`); finishing it files the obligation (`ObligationFiling`); neither imports the other. **Both processes must wire it**, or a filing closes when a person ticks and not when the worker does.
- **`work_item.completed` fires only for a SUCCESSFUL terminal state** (it used to count cancellations as work finished); `work_item.state_changed` carries `isSuccess`.
- **`filed_on` is the FILING's day (S7, 2026-10-05; it was the UTC day by the 2026-08-30 decision).** The UTC day recorded a filing after midnight in India as the previous day, exactly the on-time/late boundary. `markFiled` takes the INSTANT and writes `(instant AT TIME ZONE FILING_ZONE)::date` in the same guarded UPDATE (client entity's zone, else the filing country's, else the firm's company there, else the firm's: the fragment `filingToday` and the missed sweep read). It returns the day (null when another completion got there first); `obligation.filed` carries it. Q084's portal-day override is below.
- **A boolean query parameter is NEVER `z.coerce.boolean()`** (`Boolean("false")` is true; `?includeSettled=false` meant include, and `/calendar`'s checkbox was inert). Use `z.enum(["true","false"]).transform(…)`; `tests/api/query-booleans.test.ts` guards the class (JSON bodies and `z.coerce.number()` are fine).
- The obligation states have a screen since T3.3: `/admin/obligation-vocabulary` (counterpart of `/admin/work-vocabulary`).

### A missed deadline is a state, and the decision about it is a record (T3.3, 0055/0056)

`obligation.missed` was a REGISTERED VERB WITH NO WRITER from T3.2 (the trap 0054 closed once).

- **THE SWEEP NEEDS A DESTINATION; `needs_decision` IS NOT ONE** (a firm may have `missed` and later `disputed`, both waiting on a person). Two kinds of flag: **destinations** `is_initial` · `is_filed` · `is_missed` · `is_resolution` (at most one holder each by partial unique index, never none: the service refuses naming what would stop working; a state holds at most one of the four by CHECK); **behaviours** `is_terminal` · `needs_decision` (any number). `is_resolution` exists because "terminal and not filed" breaks the day a firm adds a third. A CHECK refuses `is_missed` on a state without `needs_decision`.
- **OPTIONS ARE ROWS and SEED `unconfirmed`:** `delivery.obligation_resolution_option`: provisional filing · late fee · nil return · not applicable, and deliberately NO "ignore" (a dismissable fork would hide what the queue exists to surface). Rows (a firm edits its list; "how many late fees this quarter" is a GROUP BY). `unconfirmed` because the demo's `docs/OPEN-QUESTIONS.md` has them as **Q57, still OPEN**.
- **`closes_without_filing` IS THE FORK, a column.** False: the filing still has to happen: back to the INITIAL state, `raise` puts it on a desk again, 0054 files it late with `daysLate`. True: into the resolution state, over.
- **`resolved_at` IS ALSO THE LOOP GUARD** (a sent-back obligation has a past statutory date forever; without it the next sweep re-flags it every morning). A column because the sweep's WHERE cannot read the ledger.
- **THE REASON IS MANDATORY AND IS NOT IN THE LOG:** service + `obligation_instance_resolution_chk` (all three columns or none; blank is not a reason). `obligation.resolved` carries the OPTION, never the reason (free text about a client; §3.4).
- **THE GO-LIVE FLOOR IS A KNOB:** `compliance_policy` (registry's ninth kind, key `default`) holds `missedFromOn`; the demo's **Q56, still OPEN** (its note calls 90 days "a working value, not a decision"). **UNSET = NO FLOOR**, the OPPOSITE default from the discount policy on purpose (there silence = zero because fail-open cannot be audited; here the failure to avoid is a hidden missed deadline).
- **`obligation:resolve` is the EIGHTH permission (0056), partner-only by default**: not `tenant:admin` (a firm may want a senior manager to decide filings without the roster), not `work:write` (this commits the CLIENT'S money and is the one decision a client may dispute).
- **`ObligationError` had no entry in `api/domainerr.ts` since T3.2** so every refusal answered 500 (second time after `StaffGradeError`).

### Editing a rule: a correction and an amendment (T6.4)

The rules screen wrote three fields; the API took a full `dueRule`, a new rule and exceptions since T3.2 with no caller, so 47 of 57 shipped rules could never start generating.

- **A CORRECTION AND AN AMENDMENT ARE DIFFERENT OPERATIONS**; `status` says which. Everything ships `unconfirmed`, so an edit there is a CORRECTION and restates in place. A period-specific legal change is an EXCEPTION (a CBDT/GST extension). **Effective-dated supersession IS built** (0057 window, 0303 handover, below); the old blocker was the generation window `today-90 … today+365` re-generating periods under a different `template_id` the re-run key could not see.
- **AN EXCEPTION IS A DATE CHANGE TOO**: adding one moves rows that already exist (March's TDS is generated eleven months before the carve-out); withdrawing restates the same way.
- **THE PERIOD SHAPE IS PART OF THE IDENTITY ONCE ROWS EXIST** (`PERIOD_SHAPE_LOCKED`, 409 not 400: well formed, refused by the filings produced). `recurrence` and `fiscal_year_start_month` decide where a period BEGINS and `period_start` is the re-run key; moving either orphans all rows and the next sweep generates a second copy. Editable exactly while the rule has produced nothing.
- **ANSWERING A RULE FILLS THE CALENDAR NOW; WRITING ONE DOES NOT.** `updateRule` generates for that template alone when the rule CROSSES into producing dates (the partner is owed the result); `createRule` does not (formula first, March carve-out second; generating on first save fills a year from a half-written rule). A brand-new rule is what the nightly sweep is for (since T6.16 that holds for a rule written in the LIBRARY only; one written in the catalogue for a service fills the calendar at once). Neither path raises tasks or flags anything missed (the sweep's), or a partner correcting a typo would put work on somebody's desk.
- **Every rule write returns a `RuleEffect`** (`restated` / `locked` / `spawned`); both exception verbs carry the first two on the event (found by reading the ledger after using the screen). Never the note.

### A rule has a window; supersession (0057, 0303)

- **`effective_from` / `effective_to` (0057)**, half-open `[from, to)`, both nullable, null on every earlier row (applying it moved no calendar). It makes an AMENDMENT expressible: the correction path edits in place; this closes a rule at a day and opens its successor on the same day. **Tested against the PERIOD START, never the due date** (August's return is August's law though due 20 September; `governsPeriod` names it once so generator and screen cannot disagree; testing the due date shifts every date by one period and looks plausible). Half-open like `client_assignment` / `party_identifier` (inclusive showed two SPOCs on a changeover day; here, two rules governing one period = two filings for one return under template ids the re-run key cannot dedupe).
- **`supersede` is ONE method and one transaction** (a closed predecessor with no successor stops clients being told of a filing they owe; an unclosed one puts every later return on twice). `supersede.integration.test.ts` proves the ROLLBACK (a colliding successor leaves the predecessor open). The predecessor is CLOSED, not retired: retiring stops it generating at all, including pre-cutover periods the 90-day reach-back has not materialised (a client onboarded last month would lose the period it was engaged for). A window says WHICH PERIODS a row governs; `is_active` whether it is on.
- **THE WINDOW ALONE LEFT EVERY RETURN TWICE: 0303 IS THE HANDOVER (2026-09-25).** The nightly sweep materialises a year ahead, so when a partner records "from 1 October the law says the 11th" the old rule has ALREADY produced October onward and the successor's spawn produced them again. Two halves:
  - **Handover:** `supersede` takes the predecessor's rows past the cutover that nobody has started (`work_item_id`, `filed_on`, `resolved_at` all null; the WHERE repeats it) and in the SAME transaction re-pins them to the successor with its dates from `scheduleFor`. Same row, same id. A successor still waiting on a fact takes the rows with their dates (first answer restates). Started/filed/decided rows STAY on the rule they were worked under and are counted (`restated` / `locked` / `spawned`).
  - **The wall:** `lineage_id` names a chain (its own id, inherited through `supersedes_id`), DERIVED by trigger on insert and refused on update. The calendar row carries it too (trigger-filled; held to its template's by composite FK `(template_id, lineage_id, tenant_id)`, 0067's pattern) and **`obligation_instance_lineage_key` is `(tenant, lineage, entity, period_start)`**: one filing per entity per period per chain; the generator's `ON CONFLICT` targets it. A WITHDRAWN row is neither handed over nor counted as kept; if restored the generator re-pins it to the rule governing its period. A catalogue link is on the CHAIN, so a successor is on every service its predecessor was.
- **`restate`'s SQL is run by an integration test, not a fake** (`jsonb_to_recordset(…::text::jsonb)`: postgres.js re-encodes a string parameter as JSON and every sweep died on it, found by RUNNING the worker). `restate.integration.test.ts` also proves the WHERE refuses a row picked up between read and write (that makes the reported count honest).

### A rule generates only once the FIRM has confirmed it (2026-09-18)

`status` was informational; the sweep generated from every active formula. Loading the first real firm: its one service was bookkeeping, which EXCLUDES GST filing in the partner's words; the 57 shipped rules name no service, so the eleven with a formula reached every live engagement at every Indian company: 98 filings, 17 tasks on an associate's desk, the client's portal listing returns the firm never took on as MISSED. A calendar row is a claim about a client; a pack cannot make one.

- `coverageOf` has a fifth answer `unconfirmed`; only `generating` generates (domain predicate, `listGenerating`, `gaps` reason `rules_unconfirmed`, `obligation-coverage.ts`; `coverage.integration.test.ts`). **A rule the firm WRITES is created `confirmed`; a rule a pack SEEDS is not.** Confirming was not enough: since T6.16 a rule reaches a client only through a LINK to a service the client bought.
- **The compliance floor clips GENERATION, not only the missed decision**: a filing whose statutory date falls before `compliance_policy.missedFromOn` is not generated (the per-period spawn's rule, T6.13).

### The silent hole: the cure is a derivation, not a setting

A recurring engagement gets one task PER PERIOD from the calendar, so `spawnForEngagements` must not also raise its own (due `effective_from + slaDays`, never completable while the engagement runs; 400 clients x 3 services = 1,200 fake tasks). **A flag can be WRONG, and wrong means SILENCE** (no engagement task and no calendar rows). So the discriminator is DERIVED: `ObligationCoverage` asks the GENERATOR'S OWN PREDICATE (an active, formula'd, not event-driven, confirmed rule with a LIVE catalogue link between its chain and the engagement's service, for the entity's country). The failure inverts: a firm with no rules is not covered and gets the old behaviour; the board can never end up empty.

- **A PER-PERIOD task asks a narrower question; a service with its own PROCESS keeps it (T6.13, then T6.16 follow-up, 2026-09-26; DECIDED by the user).** The one-time activation task asks `coveredEngagements` (skipped where the calendar dates the service's filings). A month's task ("Monthly Accounting · Sep 2026", the task a period job's steps hang under) asks `filingEngagements`: counted only when the calendar dates the filings AND the service's live published body names no `workflowTemplateKey` (read through pinned version → entry → published now, judged by `readServiceBody`). A pure "GST Compliance" is its filings; a bookkeeping service with a ten-step close that also carries TDS keeps its month's task, and the TDS returns get their own tasks beside it. Read live because the period job pins the firm's CURRENT process when it opens. `coverage.integration.test.ts` runs all four shapes (process and link, link alone, process alone, process published after signing).
- `GET /api/v1/calendar/coverage`: every live engagement the calendar produced nothing for, WITH A REASON, none an error (commonest `no_rule_names_this_service`: no filing linked in the catalogue; ordinary for advisory work). The worker logs the count every sweep; `worker:once` prints it. An empty calendar with nothing said is indistinguishable from broken software.

### What running it found

- **The batch insert never executed** (postgres.js encodes a string parameter as JSON again; `${json}::jsonb` made a jsonb STRING; `::text::jsonb` is the fix): unit tests used a fake and the integration test inserted by hand. **If you add a repository method with non-trivial SQL, run it.**
- The coverage reason was wrong on its commonest case (it required a rule to name the service; every pack rule names none): fixed to the generator's predicate, `entity_not_registered` added. **T6.16 inverted the NULL-means-every-service reading**: a pack rule names no service and generates nothing until added to one in the catalogue; hence the link lives in the catalogue and not the pack.
- **Two id-only FKs** (`service_entry_id`, `supersedes_id`) caught by `tenant-keys.integration.test.ts`, not review. `service_entry_id` is dropped since 0309; the link table's refs `(lineage_id, lineage_root, tenant_id)` and `(service_entry_id, tenant_id, service_kind)` are composite.
- Screens `/calendar` and `/admin/obligation-rules`; the chain was walked in the browser (load pack, register company, sweep, set lead time, sweep, find the GSTR-3B task at High priority, from the law).

## A filing belongs to a service: the catalogue links it, removing it withdraws (T6.16, 0309)

Before: a rule named AT MOST ONE service (`obligation_template.service_entry_id`), NULL meant "every service" (how 98 GST filings reached a bookkeeping-only client), and one service per rule meant a filing two services owe was written twice (two chains = two filings for one return). The firm's model (2026-09-26): a service lists its compliance filings in the catalogue, any number; a filing written once is offered to the next service from a list. **Four decisions, not to be re-made:** (1) a rule linked to NO service generates nothing; (2) removing a rule from a service WITHDRAWS its untouched upcoming filings for clients no other linked service covers (kept and marked, never deleted); (3) the catalogue is the one place a rule is linked to a service, the rules screen is the library; (4) built before the SFF freeze.

- **THE LINK IS ON THE CHAIN.** `delivery.obligation_rule_service`: one row per link between a rule's `lineage_id` (0303) and a catalogue ENTRY (not a version: publishing v4 of a service does not end the filing; a draft-only service can carry filings). Removing = `unlinked_at` + `unlinked_by_user_id` (both or neither); live-link uniqueness is a PARTIAL index; adding back is a new row; `bib_app` may UPDATE only the two removal columns; a trigger freezes a removed link. Proved by KEYS (0067/0303): a generated `lineage_root` copy references `obligation_template (id, lineage_id, tenant_id)` (the lineage must be a ROOT); a generated `service_kind = 'service'` references `config.entry UNIQUE (id, tenant_id, kind)` (the entry must be a SERVICE).
- **THE COLUMN IS GONE.** The data move gave each (lineage, service) a template one live system link; a NULL got none (stopped generating, which is decision 1). No calendar row touched. `service_entry_id`, its key and index were dropped (two sources of truth drift). The down restores single links (earliest where several, with a NOTICE) and REFUSES while any filing is withdrawn.
- **ONE PREDICATE, EVERY READER.** A rule generates for an engagement iff active, confirmed, has a formula, not event-driven, for the entity's country, governs the period, AND a live link joins its chain to the engagement's pinned service. `coverageOf(t, linkedServices)` gained `unlinked` (asked before `unconfirmed`: adding a pack row to a service also confirms it); `generatesFor` STATES the per-engagement form (nothing in production calls it; the generator asks `generatesDates` and `subjectsFor` joins the link); `listGenerating`, `subjectsFor` (LEFT joins became inner), `gaps` and `obligation-coverage.ts` all restate it. `FakeObligationCoverage` models `covered` and `ownProcess` as facts about the engagement's service; the obligation fakes refuse what 0309's keys refuse (a successor's id as a lineage, a non-service entry) by constraint name, and a rule store handed links with no link store wired refuses.
- **TWO SERVICES, ONE ROW PER PERIOD, ON A CHOSEN ENGAGEMENT:** the generator sorts subjects by `effective_from` then id and keeps the FIRST per (chain, entity, period) (the earliest engagement's floor lets the most periods through). `gaps` counts an engagement covered when a live row exists for its ENTITY on a chain its service carries.
- **REMOVING WITHDRAWS, IN ONE TRANSACTION WITH THE REMOVAL** (`RuleServiceLinkRepository.unlink`). `mayBeWithdrawn` (domain): untouched (`work_item_id`, `filed_on`, `resolved_at` null), upcoming on the FIRM's day (`dayInZone`, never `CURRENT_DATE`), not waiting on a decision, not settled. SQL half: no other ACTIVE engagement of the same entity whose service still has a live link AND which had started by the row's statutory date (the generator's floor `statutoryDueOn < subject.effectiveFrom`; missing until review: `pending_start` with a future `effective_from` counted as cover for September's return). Everything else KEPT and counted by reason (`keptStarted`, `keptPastDue`, `keptCoveredElsewhere`; settled history not counted); an untouched row another service covers is RE-POINTED to that service's engagement (its task routes by the service that owes it). The UPDATE repeats every condition. `withdrawn_at` / `withdrawn_by_user_id` / `withdrawn_reason` (closed set `service_unlinked`) and a CHECK that a withdrawn row is untouched.
- **THE TWO WRITERS OF A CHAIN'S CALENDAR TAKE TURNS ON ITS ROOT RULE (review, 2026-09-26).** At READ COMMITTED two orders left filings LIVE under no link (nothing withdraws; the generator only restores): two removals from two services at once each counting the other's uncommitted link as cover; a removal landing while the generator's insert ran. Both take one row lock FIRST in its own statement and read after (`obligation-chain-lock.ts`, T6.15's `step-lines.ts` argument): the chain's ROOT `obligation_template` row. Removal `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` before anything; `createIfAbsent` `FOR SHARE` on every chain its proposals name, in id order (sweeps never wait on each other; `STILL_LINKED` is asked after any removal commits). Locking the links instead deadlocks a sweep against a removal. `service-filings.integration.test.ts` forces both interleavings.
- **EVERY READER LEAVES A WITHDRAWN ROW OUT** (each a test, several watched red with the filter removed): the calendar (whatever `includeSettled` says), `listRaisable` and `attachWorkItem` (never gets a task), `listMissable` and `markMissed` (never MISSED), `decisionQueue`, `listUnstarted` and `restate`, the escalation watch and queue, nudge candidates and write, `gaps`. The PORTAL is a policy: `obligation_instance_portal_live` in 0062's guarded shape, proved by dropping it in a rolled-back transaction. `findById` is the one reader that sees it (restoring). `countFor` counts withdrawn rows (period-shape lock); `countLiveFor` does not (the restater's `locked`).
- **ADDING IT BACK RESTORES THE SAME ROWS, AND THE GENERATOR IS WHAT RESTORES**: in one transaction it first UPDATEs any WITHDRAWN untouched row its proposal holds the key of (withdrawal cleared, dates from `scheduleFor`, re-pinned to the governing rule and covering engagement), then inserts the rest `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`. Also when a client takes a second linked service. `obligation.restored` per row, carrying the instant withdrawn. **The write checks the link again** (`STILL_LINKED`: subjects were read a transaction earlier). A restored filing keeps its nudge and escalation rungs (once-indexes), never told twice; one past its date while withdrawn comes back as generated and the missed sweep asks a partner.
- **LINKING CONFIRMS, THROUGH THE LIBRARY'S OWN PATH.** `linkRule` writes the link FIRST (the spawn runs in its own transaction), confirms a rule whose chain's current row is `unconfirmed` through `applyUpdate` (one `obligation_rule.updated`, `statusFrom`/`statusTo`, its crossing spawn off), then spawns for the whole CHAIN (a predecessor still governs reach-back), and records `obligation_rule.linked` with counts. **Counts are THIS service's clients'** (a row counts only when its engagement bought this service: `CalendarSubject.serviceEntryId`, `SpawnedInstance.engagementId`). **The event is written in `finally`** (a failed fill records `calendarFilled: false`, no counts, and the error still reaches the caller; `createRule` the same for `obligation_rule.created`). A rule waiting on a formula is linked and NOT confirmed. Adding twice writes nothing twice. **A rule WRITTEN for a service fills the calendar at once** (`createRule` with `serviceEntryIds`, links in the same transaction), reversing T6.4 for that path only; T6.4's restate makes an early spawn safe.
- **Counts on the rule's events, a verb per row only where the generator writes**; `obligation_rule.linked`/`unlinked` carry ids and counts, `obligation.restored` is per row. Both also carry `serviceName` beside `serviceKey` (2026-09-26; resolved AT THE WRITE by `ServiceDirectory.findService` returning `{key, name}`, the derivation `usedIn` shows: `serviceNameSql` in `obligation-link-repository.ts`; null for an entry with no version); earlier events have none, a reader falls back to the key.
- **Routes:** `GET /api/v1/obligation-filings` (one row per chain, the rule in force, with `usedIn`), `GET /api/v1/services/{serviceEntryId}/filings`, `POST …/filings { ruleId }`, `POST …/filings/{ruleId}/unlink`. Reads `party:read`, writes `tenant:admin`. Rule rows carry `lineageId` and `usedIn: [{serviceEntryId, serviceKey, serviceName, linkedAt}]` from ONE query; `serviceEntryId` is gone from every rule write.
- **A CARD SHOWS WHAT TODAY'S FILINGS ARE DUE ON, NOT THE CHAIN'S NEWEST ROW (2026-09-26).** The catalogue's row for a chain is the open-ended one (`currentOf`); after "the law changed from April 2027" that is the SUCCESSOR, so the card said September was due the 22nd while the calendar dated it the 20th. Every rule row on all three reads carries its CHAIN read on the FIRM's today (`dayInZone`, once per request): `inForceToday` (the row whose window governs the period containing today) and `nextChange`, each `{ruleId, effectiveFrom, effectiveTo, dueRule, unresolvedReason, exceptions}` (a successor inherits no exceptions).
