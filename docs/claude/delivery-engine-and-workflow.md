# Delivery engine and workflow

Scope: the worker process and at-least-once triggers (T2.6), job grouping (0046), the orchestrator and `workflow_template` (T2.7), step desks (0301), sub-steps and review points (T6.15, 0307/0308), the agent guardrail (T2.8), cancelling a job (V2.4) and the period-end rule for period jobs (S7). Maker-checker policy and tasks are `work-items-and-review.md`; escalation sweeps are `escalation-chaser-nudge.md`.

## The delivery engine: a second process, at-least-once everywhere (T2.6)

- `npm run worker` is a SEPARATE PROCESS with its own root (`setup/worker.ts`): compliance work runs for months and pauses on human decisions. `npm run worker:once` is the same sweep with no queue in front: a different entry point, never a different rule.
- **The engine hears about work through the LEDGER, never a call.** `contract.activated` is written because a contract activated; the scheduler reads activations that have no job yet (anti-join). The commercial lane does not know an engine exists; the ledger is a durable, replayable queue.
- **EVERY TRIGGER IS AT-LEAST-ONCE** (pg-boss redelivers, the sweep re-reads events, a restart leaves a message unacked) and none may produce a second job (= second tasks = eventually a second invoice). Structural: the job id is DERIVED from its cause, `UNIQUE (tenant_id, source_kind, source_ref)` makes the second insert conflict, and the event is recorded only when a row was created. A terminal job is final; repeat work is a NEW job with its own cause.
- **`delivery.job` is not pg-boss's row** (an attempt: retried, archived, deleted, no tenant, no RLS). Ours is a business fact, tenant-scoped, outliving every retry.
- **pg-boss migrates its own tables: a real exception to the migration rule.** 0042 creates the `pgboss` schema and grants `bib_app` CREATE on **that schema and no other**; `createSchema: false` stops pg-boss issuing `CREATE SCHEMA` (fails 42501 regardless: Postgres checks the database ACL before existence). Granting CREATE on the database would let the app role create objects anywhere.
- **Date arithmetic is pure, no `Date`** (`domain/scheduler/model.ts`, ISO strings): a filing period is a calendar fact in a jurisdiction; a UTC `Date` moves a boundary by a day. A quarter aligns to the FINANCIAL year (Apr-Jun for India).

### One client, one team, one letter: one checklist (0046)

`crm.engagement` has `UNIQUE (engagement_letter_line_id)`, so a three-line letter is already three engagements and three jobs; grouping MERGES, never splits. Key **`(engagement_letter_id, entity_party_id, axis code, workflow key)`**, every part load-bearing:

- **`entity_party_id` is not negotiable** (Per Company: vCFO for four companies = four engagements; merging would put two companies' filings on one checklist). Keep it even though a letter normally names one entity (`engagement_letter_line.entity_party_id` is per LINE, NOT NULL, 0018, so the key is what makes the rule hold).
- **`engagement_letter_id`**: a job has to be able to FINISH; two letters months apart are two contracts.
- **workflow key**: two services naming two processes are two processes; merging invents an order over colliding step keys.
- **`none` merges NOTHING** (one job per engagement); a default that merged would have re-cut every firm's work.
- **Two uniqueness rules:** `job_engagement UNIQUE (tenant_id, engagement_id)` is ABSOLUTE (one engagement's work in exactly one job); `delivery.job`'s own key is PARTIAL (`WHERE status NOT IN ('done','cancelled')`: one OPEN job per group; later lines get a NEW job once the checklist finished).
- Every job has link rows, merged or not; `source_ref` is only an idempotency key and for a merged job names NO row (the group key), so `GET /api/v1/jobs` carries company, client and SERVICE NAMES in one query, and `GET /api/v1/jobs/{jobId}` exists because the list is only what is RUNNABLE. Verb `job.scope_extended` for a later line joining a checklist in flight.
- Changing `work_split_axis` re-cuts nothing existing (G2.4): a mid-year switch gives two shapes of job until the old finish.

## The orchestrator: a process a firm writes down (T2.7)

T2.6 gave a signed engagement a JOB; this gives the job a PROCESS ("how we do a GST filing" written once, walked automatically).

- **`workflow_template` is the registry's sixth kind** (deferred at T2.5: a body designed against no consumer ends up wrong). The validator refuses a CYCLE, a dependency on a missing step and a duplicate step key, at the WRITE.
- **The graph is `dependsOn`; sequential, parallel and branch fall out of ONE rule**: a step is ready when every step it depends on has settled in a way that clears its dependents. **A skip CLEARS its dependents; a failure does NOT** (two terminal-but-not-done states; collapsing them strands a graph or releases work waiting on a machine that gave up). `cascadeSkips` is the other half of a branch.
- **AGENTS ARE OPTIONAL, a product decision.** `human` is the ordinary executor; a firm can run entirely on human steps TODAY and a step becomes an agent's by changing one field. **A template may say `agent` before any agent exists; the job does not fail, the step waits for a person.** `NoAgentsBuiltYet` is an honest implementation of a true statement, so it is NOT gated on config like the dev resolver / logging mailer / attested signer (those are stubs for something that must exist before launch).
- **The pin is at the JOB.** `delivery.job.template_version_id` is written ONCE (`WHERE template_version_id IS NULL`, in SQL, so two workers cannot both set it) and never re-resolved: editing a process must never rewrite a job already walking it. **The SERVICE is read LIVE** (letter line's pinned version → ENTRY → what is published now): a client agreed service, price and scope, not our internal steps. (It started as the opposite and was a real bug: engagements signed before a firm's first process pinned a service version naming no template.)
- **`delivery.job_step` is rows, not a blob**: dashboards ("how many stuck on review") need no scan, work is handed out by an indexed column, and **maker != checker can only be a DB constraint if who performed a step is a column** (`performed_by`).
- **A skipped step is kept and shown.** The schema INSISTS on a reason for a skip and a failure and forbids one elsewhere. The ledger carries the FACT, never the prose.
- **AN UNRESOLVED FACT KEEPS THE STEP**, under EVERY operator: an extra review costs an hour, a missing one a filing. The negative operators are the subtle half ("jurisdiction is not IN" is *unknown*, not true when the jurisdiction is blank), so the null check comes before the operator. Read `evaluateCondition` before "simplifying" it into an else.
- **A CONDITION IS JUDGED AGAIN AFTER THE STEP OPENS (0304, 2026-09-25).** Facts are read LIVE, so a step could sit on a desk for weeks after the firm's rule stopped applying. Every walk re-judges a `blocked` or `running` step (`withdrawOpenedSteps`) and skips it when the condition DEFINITELY fails and **no work is recorded on it** (`performed_by` null, no undecided machine proposal, no sub-step marked, done or N/A: "work that exists is never discarded because a catalogue field changed"). The row keeps `started_at` (0304 relaxed the timestamp CHECK: a skipped step may carry the day it OPENED, never a finish time) and its `assignee_user_id`; `job.step_skipped` adds `from`, `afterStart`, assignee id. Ticking it late answers `STEP_NO_LONGER_NEEDED` (409). `cascadeSkips` still takes only pending steps (complete, not a gap).
- **`listRunnable` includes `blocked`** (leaving it out was the second real bug: a job waiting on a person vanished from the work board and the sweep). Only `done` and `cancelled` are excluded.
- **Step conditions test a CLOSED set of facts** (`recurrence`, `jurisdiction`) with a CLOSED set of operators (`equals`, `not_equals`, `in`, `not_in`). A little expression language was refused. Adding a fact = a member of `StepFact`, a case in the orchestrator's resolver, a line of docs.
- **No `gte` / `lte` until the first NUMERIC FACT (T3.2).** The firm's compliance list (2026-08-30) makes a threshold its commonest driver ("turnover > ₹1 Cr", "AATO ≥ ₹5 Cr", "net worth ≥ ₹500 Cr") but nothing stores a turnover, employee count, paid-up capital or listed status; a numeric operator would be an inert control. **The SHAPE moved, the operators did not:** `{ fact, op, value }` replaced `{ fact, equals }` the day the need was known (changing shape later would orphan every published body); adding an operator later is backward compatible. **The legacy `{ fact, equals }` is frozen into every older version; `readStepCondition` normalises it to `op: "equals"`.**

### Whose desk a step sits on (0301, 2026-09-18)

`performed_by` said who DID a step, nothing said who was EXPECTED. The firm's process names "Analyst / Associate / Senior Associate" on eight steps and "Manager" on the ninth. A template step carries a `performer` BAND `{minGradeRank, minGradeCode, maxGradeRank, maxGradeCode}` (rank compared, code read, either end open, null = anybody); a job step carries `assignee_user_id`. At `createSteps` every human step inside its band defaults to the job's OWNER under `resolveOwner` (SPOC, Key Manager, signing partner; 2026-09-27; `StepDeskResolver.ownerFactsFor` gathers facts because the two use cases must not import each other); outside the band it starts on nobody's desk. `assignStep` takes / hands over / releases, checks roster and band, writes `job.step_assigned` with `how`. `completeStep` refuses somebody else's step (`NotYourStep`: take it first) and a performer outside the band (`PerformerOutOfBand`). `describeSteps` computes `viewer.mayComplete` where the refusal is, so the button and the 409 cannot disagree; `my-steps` is the desk. `tests/orchestrator/desk.test.ts`. **`work:write` (0045)** is what a person needs to say a step is done (its own permission: it gates the record of who did the work).

### A step's own lists: sub-steps and review points (T6.15, 0307, 2026-09-25)

The firm's process has bullets under every step; they asked for a checkbox per line with the step shut until every line is settled. A template step carries two lists of `{ key, label }`, TWO because two different people tick them:

- **`checklist` = SUB-STEPS, ticked by whoever does the step**; all before Done.
- **`reviewPoints` = ticked by whoever CHECKS it**; all before Approve. Decided 2026-09-18, not to be re-decided: on step 8 the manager's part is the `manager_review` policy, so "Check: P&L movements" is the MANAGER's. The validator refuses review points on a step that names no check.
- Absent reads `[]` (published bodies are FROZEN; the editor always writes both). Keys follow the step-key rule, unique within ONE list, at most 40 lines, labels 1-200 chars trimmed. **Sub-steps are refused on an `agent` step** (nothing would tick them). Second wall: a step with sub-steps is never committed unattended whatever the confidence (`approvalRequired` set in `takeReadySteps`), and accepting the machine's answer is held to the list in `decideProposal`.
- **"NOT APPLICABLE" is allowed and needs a reason** (user, 2026-09-25; the firm's lists say "WHERE INCLUDED", "WHERE APPLICABLE"): trimmed, <= 500 chars, `NOT_APPLICABLE_NEEDS_A_REASON` (400) by service AND CHECK; a reason with a plain tick is dropped. **"Blank" is what JavaScript's `trim()` strips (0308)**: 0307's `btrim` stripped spaces only, so a newline-and-tab reason passed the wall the service refused. The pattern names code points rather than `[[:space:]]` (locale-dependent); the integration test holds the two definitions together character by character. 0307 was fixed forward (applied with marks in it; a down destroys them).
- **Marks are `delivery.job_step_check`**: one row per (list, line), absent row = open, unticking sets `outcome` NULL, no DELETE grant. A SUB-STEP mark is keyed on the step and survives a send-back; a REVIEW mark is keyed on the APPROVAL, so a re-check starts with every point open and "this took two goes" keeps each round's ticks. The review mark's step must be its check's step, a WALL: 0307 added `UNIQUE (id, job_step_id, tenant_id)` to `step_approval` (0063's move for `client_ask`). Labels copied onto the row at mark time; screens read the pinned template's label and order.
- **Gates:** `completeStep` and `decideProposal` (accept and correct, never reject) refuse `SUB_STEPS_OPEN` (409), naming what is left (five, then "and N more"). `completeStep` checks after desk/band rules; **`decideProposal` applies no desk or band rule at all** (as since T2.8) and checks before the verdict is written. `decideCheck` refuses APPROVE with `REVIEW_POINTS_OPEN` (409) and **never refuses Send back** (the checker may find the problem at point three); it also refuses Approve with a sub-step open, with a sentence for the CHECKER (a wall under a wall). Ticks are LOCKED while a check is open (`AWAITING_CHECK`) and once done (`STEP_NOT_WAITING`). Who ticks: a sub-step, anybody who could tick the step (`assertMayDo`); a review point, anybody `eligibilityOf` lets check it (the MAKER can never tick their own). A job whose pinned version names no list or no longer reads is never gated (a bad config row must not strand a job).
- **EVERY GATE IS ALSO A LOCK** (found in review, 2026-09-26): read-then-move in two calls is check-then-act. The write carries its own precondition under ONE row lock per list, taken first in its own transaction and read AFTER (`infrastructure/postgres/step-lines.ts`): a sub-step mark, a guarded step move (`transition(…, subStepKeys)`) and a guarded check opening (`openIfAbsent(…, subStepKeys)`) lock the STEP's row; a review mark and a guarded Approve (`decide(…, settled)`) lock the CHECK's row. `FOR NO KEY UPDATE`. **A subquery in the UPDATE would not do** (a waiting statement re-checks that row but reads other tables from its starting snapshot). A mark refused under the lock answers null/false and the service says which refusal (`whySubStepsLocked`, `whyNotFinished`, `whyNotDecided`). A sub-step is closed while its step has a check pending OR APPROVED; a rejected check closes nothing. The guard rides on the check OPENING (the list is still open after the blocked-to-blocked transition); a refused opening puts `performed_by` back. **One stated gap:** a tick taken back between `decideProposal`'s verdict and its move refuses the move after the verdict is written. `step-checks.integration.test.ts` proves each lock by holding the other side's transaction open.
- **A MACHINE'S COMMITTED ANSWER ON A CHECKED STEP IS STILL CHECKED** (0048's intent, built 2026-09-26): 0048 made `submitted_by` nullable for an agent-committed step, but the engine never opened the check. `takeReadySteps` now leaves a committed step with a policy `blocked` with a check open and no maker. Dormant while `NoAgentsBuiltYet` is wired.
- **`describeOne` carries the step's undecided proposal** (every write route used to answer `proposal: null`, so the job page dropped Accept / Correct / Reject and offered a Done the API refused). `tests/api/step-checks-routes.test.ts` holds it.
- **OPEN, NOT DECIDED:** does whoever ticked a sub-step count as a maker? Maker != checker compares with `submitted_by` (who pressed Done), so tick-all then hand over lets the ticker approve. Unreachable on the firm's process (doer bands stop at Senior Associate; `manager_review` needs a Manager); extending the maker is a rule about independence for the firm to state.
- **No ledger event per tick** (0051's reason: ten per step bury the transitions). `job.step_advanced` carries `subSteps: { total, done, notApplicable }`, and `output.approved`/`output.rejected` carry `reviewPoints` the same way; counts only, never a label or reason.
- Reads: `JobStep.subSteps` + `subStepsOpen` (the count the 409 uses; `viewer.mayComplete` keeps meaning WHO), `StepCheck.reviewPoints` + `reviewPointsOpen` on a job's checks and the firm's queue (marker's name joined in the same query; queue rows carry the job's pinned version, no per-row lookup). Routes `PUT /api/v1/jobs/{jobId}/steps/{stepKey}/sub-steps/{itemKey}` and `PUT /api/v1/checks/{approvalId}/review-points/{itemKey}`, body `{ outcome: "done" | "not_applicable" | null, note? }`, both `work:write`, the marker always the session's user. Tests `tests/orchestrator/step-checks*.test.ts`, `tests/config/step-checklist.test.ts`.

## The agent guardrail: built before there is an agent (T2.8)

`NoAgentsBuiltYet` is still wired. This built the RAILS (guardrails first, then capabilities).

- **The guardrail is a CHECK in 0047, not an orchestrator rule** (`build2.md`: *a rule that lives in a form is a rule that a script, an import or an AI agent walks straight past*, and here the rule is ABOUT agents). A CHECK cannot read config, so **`delivery.agent_run` copies the thresholds that applied** onto every row (like `job.split_code`, `job_step.executor`); "why was this allowed" survives the firm raising its bar.
- **A machine acts unattended only STRICTLY above the bar**: `>` not `>=`, so **1.0 means never**. Do not tidy that comparison.
- **Three answers:** `committed` · `queued` (the machine answered, a person says whether it stands) · `escalated` (not sure enough). "Commit or don't" would waste every usable draft.
- **`confidence_tier` is the registry's SEVENTH kind**: a run PINS the version it was judged under (*why did this commit on its own?* has the true answer "the bar was 0.95 that day"). (`staff_grade` went the other way: nobody asks what a rung meant last March.)
- **NOTHING IS SEEDED; shipping this changed no firm's behaviour.** No published tier = `ASK_A_PERSON` `(autoCommitAt 1, reviewAt 0)`, same two numbers, no special case (cf. no discount policy = ZERO). `SUGGESTED_TIERS` (0.95 / 0.70) is a starting point a firm publishes deliberately and the numbers are **not the client's** (the demo records them as inherited from another vendor).
- **THE TEMPLATE OUTRANKS THE NUMBERS**: `agent_with_approval` never commits whatever the confidence. `approval_required` is stored on the run so the CHECK can express it; `runsAMachine` is a separate question from `waitsForAPerson` (conflating them was a real bug: the step was blocked before `agents.run`, so the person had nothing to approve).
- **`proposed` and `applied` both survive forever** (`build2.md` §5.2; the accuracy signal that could justify moving a threshold). Two CHECKs: a `corrected` must ACTUALLY differ, an `accepted` must not. A rejection must say why and does NOT finish the step.
- A trigger refuses edits to proposal, confidence, thresholds, decision or provenance (only a person's verdict arrives later). `model` and `prompt_version` NOT NULL from the start.
- **Ticking Done behind a proposal is refused** (`PROPOSAL_UNDECIDED`).

## Cancelling a job (V2.4)

`POST /jobs/{id}/cancel` (`work:write`): the job's open TASKS move first, to the firm's own cancelled state read by its flags (`cancelledState`: active, terminal, not a success) through `moveToState`; a firm with none is refused (`NO_CANCELLED_STATE`) before anything moves; then the job row, guarded in the write (`NOT IN ('done','cancelled')`; 0041's CHECK makes a never-started job start and finish at the cancel instant); then its open STEPS, skipped with "The job was cancelled." (0304 keeps an opened step's start). One `job.cancelled` with the withdrawn step keys and counts. The job keeps its engagement links, so the period is never spawned again. The checkers' queue leaves out a cancelled job's pending checks. The orchestrator reaches tasks through the `JobTasks` port that `WorkItemService` satisfies.

## A period job's task waits for its period to end (S7, 2026-10-05)

A period job's steps are laid out when its period ENDS, so before then `JOB_STEPS_OPEN` held nothing and a task finished inside its own month filed that month's returns with no checklist run. `assertJobStepsSettled` now asks `periodEndOf` first (the orchestrator: a job's `period_end` when it is an `engagement_period` job, else null) and refuses Done and Approve while the FIRM's day (staff work) is on or before it: `JOB_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED` (409, "This job is for a period that ends on …"). Cancelling is never held. Closed on Shrestha's word. Test `tests/workitem/service.test.ts`.
